Brand direction 2 of 3: The Uptown original

A familiar face.A character all its own.

Think of the bookstore with the cat in the window and the café that knows your order. Birchwood should feel like that: well read, a little independent, and clearly part of the block.

Oxblood ink on a reading-room blue.

The colors of a well-loved library: oxblood bindings, buttercream pages, a pale blue room to read in. Apricot and charcoal come out for the details.

A typical page uses each color in roughly these proportions, from most to least.

Reading-room blue#DCE4E8

Page backgrounds and wide open space.

Oxblood#5C2433

The wordmark, headlines and primary actions.

Buttercream#F6EBCF

Paper: letters, notices and type on oxblood.

Dusty blue#A9BDC8

Quiet panels and illustration.

Charcoal#2A2627

Body text and everyday detail.

Apricot#EBA17A

Small warm moments. Use it as a surface, never as text.

Text pairings that pass WCAG AA: oxblood or charcoal on the light colors, and buttercream on oxblood or charcoal. Oxblood on the page blue measures 9.4:1, and buttercream on oxblood 10.1:1.

A bookish voice that still gets to the point.

Caslon has set books for three hundred years, and that history is what we borrow. A plain, sturdy grotesk handles anything residents need to act on.

A name worth signing.

The wordmark leads. The B monogram signs off, like a bookplate pressed inside a front cover: a stem for the building, two bowls for the floors, and a small cross-street up in the corner.

The wordmark is set in live type for this prototype. Final artwork would be redrawn and outlined, with the B and the d given a little more character.

Out on the block, and in the mailbox.

A blade sign that hangs over the sidewalk like a bookstore’s, and a welcome letter that reads like a note from a neighbor.

A front door that reads like a front page.

A homepage that opens with a welcome, not a leasing banner: one generous headline, two clear next steps, and the shelf of stories alongside.
