Brand direction 03: the bright, friendly local

Hello,neighbor.

An identity for Birchwood Property Group built around an open door. Warm color, rounded letters and plain words, so it feels like the people down the hall rather than a company far away.

Not a luxury label, not a corporate handshake. Just a clear, friendly way to say welcome.

hello

Mostly daylight, with a teal backbone.

Five colors, used in roughly these amounts. Soft white and teal do the everyday work. Marigold is the porch light: a small amount goes a long way.

Soft white#F6FAF8, page and paper
Deep teal#17555A, name, headlines, actions
Aqua#CCE6E0, quiet panels
Marigold#F3B53A, warmth
Graphite#21343A, text
Each band's size shows its intended share of a typical page.Every text pairing on this page meets WCAG AA contrast.
  • Teal speaksUse teal for the name, headlines and the one action that matters most on a page.
  • Marigold glowsUse marigold as a surface behind dark text, never as thin text on white.
  • White breathesLeave more white than feels necessary. It keeps a busy notice easy to scan.

Round enough to smile, clear enough to read.

Fredoka gives headlines soft corners and a little bounce. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, designed for readers with low vision, carries every notice, form and lease summary.

  • Display, Fredoka 600Come on in
  • Heading, Fredoka 600Your new keys
  • Body, Atkinson 400Short sentences, plain verbs and the detail you need first.
  • Small, Atkinson 700Bold for small text, never all capitals.

Offline, the page falls back to Arial Rounded or Trebuchet for headlines and Verdana for text, so proportions will shift a little.

Two shapes make an open door.

An arched frame and a door leaf, offset just enough to read as open. It works in one color, holds up at icon size and sits beside a rounded, lowercase-friendly wordmark.

Primary, deep teal on aqua BirchwoodProperty Group The arch behind it is the shape of the mark's frame, reused for panels and images.
Reversed, one color BirchwoodProperty Group
Small sizes and app icon

The frame is drawn from the arched entries common on older neighborhood buildings. The leaf steps forward from it, the way a door does when someone opens it for you. Shown here in two colors only to explain the parts; the symbol is always used in one color.
