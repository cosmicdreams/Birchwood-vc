How people will use the Birchwood website

Six draft workflows: one map of the whole site, then one for each job the site has to do. They are a starting point for conversation, built from a short brief, so most steps are educated guesses.

Under each diagram is a list of the assumptions it makes. The questions that would confirm or correct them are in questions.md. Each diagram is also its own file in the workflows folder, so it can be edited on its own.

EveryoneThe whole site at a glance

Four groups of people use three parts of the site, and everything they do reads or writes the same shared records. A lead from the public site becomes an applicant, then a tenant, and the same person record follows them the whole way.

flowchart LR
  subgraph People
    P([Public visitor])
    T([Tenant])
    S([Office staff])
    M([Management])
  end
  subgraph Site[Birchwood website]
    MK[Public site: listings and marketing]
    TP[Tenant portal]
    OPS[Staff workspace]
  end
  subgraph Records[Shared records]
    CRM[(Leads and CRM)]
    TEN[(Tenants and leases)]
    PAY[(Rent and payments)]
    MNT[(Maintenance requests)]
    PROP[(Properties and units)]
    VEN[(Supers and vendors)]
  end
  P --> MK
  T --> TP
  S --> OPS
  M --> OPS
  MK --> CRM
  MK -.-> AN[(Analytics)]
  TP --> TEN
  TP --> PAY
  TP --> MNT
  OPS --> CRM
  OPS --> TEN
  OPS --> PAY
  OPS --> MNT
  OPS --> PROP
  OPS --> VEN
  MNT --> VEN
  • Assumes one website with three areas rather than separate tools, and one shared set of records.
  • Assumes office staff and management use the same workspace with different permissions.
  • Supers appear here as records, not as users. They may need their own sign-in.

Office staffKeeping the tenant list right

The tenant list is the core record of the business. It starts when an applicant is approved and ends when a move-out is settled. Every change in between, from a new roommate to a renewal, should land in one place.

flowchart TD
  A[Applicant from the CRM] --> B[Application and screening]
  B -->|Approved| C[Lease signed]
  B -->|Declined| X[Applicant notified, lead closed]
  C --> D[Tenant record created: unit, lease dates, rent, contacts]
  D --> E[Move-in: keys, inspection, portal invite]
  E --> F[Active tenancy]
  F --> G{Something changes?}
  G -->|Roommate, contact details, pet| H[Staff updates the record]
  H --> F
  F --> I{Renewal window opens}
  I -->|Renews| J[New lease term]
  J --> F
  I -->|Moving out| K[Notice to vacate]
  K --> L[Move-out inspection, deposit settled]
  L --> Z[Record archived, unit goes back to listings]
  • Assumes screening (credit, background, income) happens inside this process, possibly through an outside service.
  • Assumes leases are signed electronically.
  • Assumes past tenants are archived, not deleted, so their history stays available.

Office staffCollecting rent

The goal is a high, steady collection rate. Most rent should arrive with nothing more than a reminder. The rest of the work is spotting a late payment early and getting it resolved before it becomes an eviction.

flowchart TD
  A[Rent posted to each ledger on schedule] --> B[Reminder before the due date]
  B --> C{Paid by the due date?}
  C -->|Yes| D[Receipt sent, ledger reconciled]
  C -->|No| E[Grace period]
  E --> F{Paid during grace?}
  F -->|Yes| D
  F -->|No| G[Late fee and late notice]
  G --> H{Tenant responds?}
  H -->|Asks for help| I[Payment plan agreed]
  I --> J[Plan instalments tracked]
  J --> D
  H -->|No response| K[Staff call or visit]
  K --> L{Resolved?}
  L -->|Yes| D
  L -->|No| N[Formal notice, legal process]
  D -.-> R[(Collection rate dashboard)]
  G -.-> R
  N -.-> R
  • Assumes tenants can pay online and that some still pay by check or money order.
  • Assumes a grace period and a late fee exist. Both are set by lease terms and local law.
  • Assumes payment plans are allowed and tracked inside the site.

ManagementBringing in a new property

A new building goes live only when its ramp-up checklist is complete and someone in management signs it off. Every unfinished item has a named owner, so nothing stalls quietly.

flowchart TD
  A[Property acquired or management contract signed] --> B[Property record created]
  B --> C[Units added: layout, rent, status]
  C --> D[Existing tenants and leases imported]
  D --> E[Super and vendors assigned]
  E --> F[Payments set up: bank account, rent schedule]
  F --> G[Inspection and condition baseline]
  G --> H[Photos, descriptions, listing copy]
  H --> I[Existing tenants welcomed: letter and portal invite]
  I --> J{Ramp-up checklist complete?}
  J -->|Gaps remain| K[Each gap gets an owner and a date]
  K --> J
  J -->|Complete| L[Management sign-off: property is live]
  L --> M[30, 60 and 90-day reviews]
  • Assumes Birchwood both owns properties and may manage some for other owners.
  • The order of the steps is a guess. Several can probably run in parallel.
  • Your brief left a second management item blank. There is a question about it in questions.md.

TenantUsing the tenant portal

Tenants come to the portal for four things: what their rental includes, the dates that matter, paying rent, and reaching the super. Emergencies skip the form and go straight to a phone line.

flowchart TD
  A[Tenant signs in] --> B{What do they need?}
  B --> C[My rental: lease, unit details, house rules, documents]
  B --> D[Important dates: rent due, inspections, renewal window]
  B --> E[Pay rent, see balance and history]
  B --> F[Contact the super]
  D --> D1[Renewal reminders 90, 60 and 30 days out]
  D1 --> D2{Renew?}
  D2 -->|Yes| D3[Sign renewal online]
  D2 -->|No| D4[Give notice to vacate]
  F --> F1{Urgent?}
  F1 -->|Emergency: water, heat, lockout| F2[Call the emergency line]
  F1 -->|Routine| F3[Maintenance request with photos]
  F3 --> F4[Super triages]
  F4 --> F5{Can the super fix it?}
  F5 -->|Yes| F6[Super schedules a visit]
  F5 -->|No| F7[Vendor dispatched]
  F6 --> F8[Work done, tenant confirms]
  F7 --> F8
  F8 --> F9[Request closed, cost logged against the vendor]
  • Assumes each building has one super who is the first stop for every request.
  • Assumes reminder timing of 90, 60 and 30 days. The real notice periods depend on lease terms and local law.
  • Assumes tenants can see vendor visit times but not vendor costs.

PublicFrom visitor to lead

The public site has one job: turn an interested visitor into a lead the leasing team can follow up. Analytics records where each lead came from, so marketing spend can be judged by leases signed, not by clicks.

flowchart TD
  A[Visitor arrives: search, ads, social, building signage] --> B[Browses properties and neighborhood pages]
  B --> C{Interested?}
  C -->|Not yet| D[Leaves, or signs up for listing alerts]
  C -->|Yes| E[Inquiry or tour request]
  E --> F[Lead created in the CRM with its source]
  F --> G[Leasing staff assigned, follow-up within a target time]
  G --> H[Tour]
  H --> I{Applies?}
  I -->|Yes| J[Application: hands off to the tenant list workflow]
  I -->|No| K[Lead nurtured or closed with a reason]
  A -.-> AN[(Analytics)]
  E -.-> AN
  F -.-> AN
  J -.-> AN
  • Assumes the website captures leads into a CRM, either one built in or an existing product.
  • Assumes tours are booked by people, not self-scheduled online.
  • Assumes "analytics" means both website traffic and the funnel from lead to lease.
