Good bones. Warm welcome.

An identity with its feet on the sidewalk: sturdy geometry, a softened edge, and the everyday warmth of a place that feels like yours.

Color

Evergreen leads and limestone gives it room. Sage softens the quiet surfaces, cream is kept for paper and reversed type, and brick shows up only where a real building would: in the illustration and the wall behind a sign.

Bar widths show roughly how much of a typical layout each color covers.

  • Evergreen

    #24483E
    The name, headlines and buttons. 8.7:1 on limestone.

  • Limestone

    #ECEFE7
    The everyday canvas. Cool enough to let evergreen feel warm.

  • Sage

    #C9D6C5
    Bands, panels and quiet backgrounds. Ink reads at 10:1.

  • Cream

    #F7F2E8
    Letters, signs and type reversed out of evergreen.

  • Ink

    #1F2622
    Body text and the stoop under the doorway.

  • Brick

    #8E4632
    Illustration and sign walls only. Cream reads at 6.1:1.

Type

Albert Sans grew out of Scandinavian modernist signage, so it reads clearly on a door and still feels friendly in a note. One family carries everything; weight and spacing do the work.

Bb

Offline, the page falls back to Helvetica Neue or Arial. Final signage would use outlined artwork, not live type.

Logo

A doorway hidden in a sturdy B: one straight spine, two softened bowls, and an opening at the base. The lowercase wordmark sits close and unhurried beside it.

The symbol is original SVG artwork. The wordmark is a live-type spacing study, not final lettering.
